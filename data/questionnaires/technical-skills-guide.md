# Technical Skills Proficiency Framework

A guide for assessing and self-reporting proficiency levels in risk, audit, and control technical skills.

---

## Proficiency Scale Overview

| Level | Label | Description |
|-------|-------|-------------|
| **0** | No Experience | No exposure or experience in this area |
| **1** | Developing | Has tested, reviewed, or observed existing frameworks. External or support perspective. Works under supervision. |
| **2** | Proficient | Can operate, maintain, and improve established frameworks. Works independently within existing structures. |
| **3** | Advanced | Can implement and adapt frameworks using established methodologies. Builds and leads for specific contexts. |
| **4** | Expert | Can design from first principles. Creates methodologies. Leads enterprise-wide transformations. Recognised authority. |

---

## Key Differentiating Dimensions

### Perspective
| Level | Typical Perspective |
|-------|---------------------|
| 1 | External auditor / reviewer / tester |
| 2 | Internal operator / maintainer |
| 3 | Implementer / project lead |
| 4 | Designer / strategist / function builder |

### Ownership
| Level | Relationship to Framework |
|-------|---------------------------|
| 1 | Tested or reviewed someone else's work |
| 2 | Inherited and maintained existing frameworks |
| 3 | Built using established templates and methodologies |
| 4 | Designed the template or methodology itself |

### Scope
| Level | Typical Scope |
|-------|---------------|
| 1 | Individual controls or processes |
| 2 | Control environment or process area |
| 3 | Function-wide implementation |
| 4 | Enterprise-wide architecture |

---

## Questions to Determine Your Level

Ask yourself:

1. Did I **test/review** it or **build** it?
2. Did I **inherit** the framework or **create** it?
3. Was I working **from templates** or **designing** the templates?
4. Was this **one area/process** or **enterprise-wide**?
5. Did I **operate within** methodology or **define** the methodology?
6. Was I the **executor** or the **architect**?

---

## Evidence Indicators by Level

### Level 1 - Developing
- Performed testing under supervision
- Documented findings others identified
- Supported senior team members
- Familiar with concepts but limited hands-on experience
- Worked on single controls or processes

### Level 2 - Proficient
- Works independently on BAU activities
- Maintains existing frameworks
- Manages relationships with stakeholders
- Produces regular reporting
- Handles standard situations without escalation

### Level 3 - Advanced
- Implemented programs in new areas
- Designed methodologies within guidelines
- Led teams or workstreams
- Built frameworks using established templates
- Trained others on the subject

### Level 4 - Expert
- Designed from first principles
- Created the methodology others use
- Enterprise-wide scope
- Recognised as authority internally/externally
- Led transformation programs
- Board or executive level engagement

## Common Over-Claiming Indicators

Be honest with yourself. These phrases often indicate a lower level than claimed:

| Phrase | Likely Actual Level |
|--------|---------------------|
| "I was involved in..." | Level 1-2 |
| "I maintained..." | Level 2 |
| "I implemented using [methodology]..." | Level 3 |
| "I designed from scratch..." | Level 4 |
| "I built the function..." | Level 4 |

## Technical Skills Definitions

### 1. Internal Controls (SOX/Non-SOX)

*Design, implementation, and testing of internal control frameworks including SOX compliance*

| Level | Definition |
|-------|------------|
| 0 | No internal controls experience |
| 1 | Has tested controls as external or internal auditor; documented findings and exceptions; understands control objectives, assertions, and testing methodologies |
| 2 | Owns control testing program; maintains control documentation and evidence repository; tracks remediation; coordinates with external auditors; manages control owners |
| 3 | Implemented control frameworks in new areas or entities; designed test plans and control matrices; established control libraries; led remediation programs; SOX coordination |
| 4 | Built SOX environment from scratch; designed control taxonomy and methodology; led IPO or listing readiness; created control framework architecture; recognised as controls authority |

**Interview Questions:**
- Walk me through a control you tested - what was the objective, how did you test it, and what was the outcome?
- Have you inherited a control environment or built one? Describe the difference in your experience.
- Tell me about a time you identified a control gap. How did you remediate it?

### 2. Risk Appetite & Risk Framework

*Development and operationalisation of risk appetite statements and enterprise risk frameworks*

| Level | Definition |
|-------|------------|
| 0 | No risk appetite or framework experience |
| 1 | Has reviewed risk appetite statements; understands concept of risk tolerance and capacity; tested adherence to risk limits; familiar with risk categories |
| 2 | Operationalises existing risk appetite; reports on risk metrics and limit breaches; maintains risk framework documentation; escalates threshold breaches; supports risk committees |
| 3 | Developed and cascaded risk appetite statements from board level to business units; translated qualitative appetite into quantitative limits; built risk reporting and dashboards |
| 4 | Designed enterprise risk framework from first principles; created risk taxonomy and appetite methodology; authored board-level risk appetite statements; built risk function from scratch |

**Interview Questions:**
- How would you explain risk appetite vs risk tolerance to a business stakeholder?
- Describe how you've seen risk appetite cascade from board level to operational limits.
- Have you ever drafted a risk appetite statement? Walk me through the process.

### 3. Risk & Control Self-Assessment (RCSA)

*Design and facilitation of RCSA processes and risk identification methodologies*

| Level | Definition |
|-------|------------|

| 1 | Has participated in RCSA workshops as contributor; understands risk identification and rating process; contributed to risk registers; familiar with inherent vs residual risk |
| 2 | Facilitates RCSA sessions with business areas; maintains and updates risk registers; coordinates risk ratings with control owners; produces RCSA reports; challenges assessments |
| 3 | Implemented RCSA methodology in new business areas; designed workshop templates and facilitation guides; trained RCSA facilitators; built risk libraries and taxonomies |

**Interview Questions:**
- What's your role typically been in RCSA workshops - facilitator, contributor, or designer?
- How do you challenge a business area that consistently rates their risks as low?
- Have you ever had to implement RCSA in an area that didn't have it before?

## Important Note for Candidates

**These skill ratings will be explored during interviews.**

When self-assessing your proficiency level, be prepared to:

1. **Provide specific examples** demonstrating your level
2. **Describe your exact role** - were you the lead, contributor, or observer?
3. **Explain the scope** - was this one process, a department, or enterprise-wide?
4. **Articulate the outcome** - what did you deliver and what was the impact?

Interviewers will probe the difference between:
- Testing vs building
- Maintaining vs implementing
- Working within frameworks vs designing frameworks
- Individual contribution vs leadership

**Be honest** - it's better to accurately represent Level 2 with strong examples than to claim Level 4 and be unable to demonstrate it.
